## What worked on KarenReacts (public view counts)
| Video | Views | Why it likely worked |
|---|---|---|
| ME & MY FRIENDS REACTING TO KATSEYE "M.I.A" (Lollapalooza '25) | 25K | Friends on camera (group energy) + live festival performance |

| Typical solo MV reaction | 74-900 | Crowded; big reactors get there first |

**What this says (repeatable vs one-off):**
- Repeatable: **you plus other people, reacting to live performances** beat solo MV reactions by 20-50x. Your face/personality is the product, not the song.
- Repeatable: putting **"singer"** in the title performed 3-6x better than generic reaction titles.
- Likely one-off: the Lollapalooza videos also rode KATSEYE's moment. Do not assume any group video repeats 25K.
- Streams: release-day streams for big acts got 1.5-2.9K. Generic "Day 14 of #StreamVember" streams got 28-74. **A stream needs a reason to click**, not just a date.
- Consistency: uploads clustered in bursts (Oct 2025, Nov 2025, Mar 2026, Apr 2026), then nothing. This is the trust problem you described, and it is visible in the data.

## What worked on Karen Tafa Two (vlog channel)
- Best: Snowdrop reaction (7.8K), "I ate like Song Jia" diet/workout (4.5K), Snowdrop eps (2.8K), a software-engineer day-in-the-life (1.5K).
- 2026 vlogs/self-improvement videos: 26-48 views. Generic titles and no named hook.
- Pattern: **a named person/show in the title** drove views; "my life" content with no hook did not. For the artist brand that means attaching vlogs to a hook (a challenge, a transformation, a song), not a plain day-in-my-life.
